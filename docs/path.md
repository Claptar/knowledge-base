## Plan, not record

A plan is exactly what [questions.md](questions.md#harvest-dont-design) forbids a topic file from
being — structure written before the work — so it is quarantined here. A node is not a page and
finishing one creates no file; its question is my reconstruction of what *would* force the concept
and reaches the Live table in [questions.md](questions.md#live) only once I hold it. The path is
wrong in places by construction: delete what stops biting.

## How to use it

Nodes are ordered by dependency, not by date. **Needs** lists the nodes that must come first;
anything with its needs met is a legal next session. A node is done on the usual criterion — the
*how could I have come up with this?* answer exists, in my own words, for the concept that node
names — and not on having read the source.

Two nodes are already live questions and are marked **[live]**.

## Layer 1 — conditioning and information

The layer everything else rests on, and the one where my existing anchors are strongest. Source
throughout: Williams, [Probability with Martingales](resources/probability.md).

### 1. Conditional expectation as an orthogonal projection

**Needs** nothing.

Conditioning on an event of probability zero is meaningless, yet $E[X \mid Y]$ for continuous $Y$
is used constantly. What object is actually being defined, and why is it specified by a property
it satisfies rather than by a formula?

**Anchor.** The conjecture to test is that $E[\cdot \mid \mathcal{G}]$ on $L^2$ is the same
construction as PCA-as-Gram-matrix with a different subspace. If it is, the tower property should
fall out as nested projections with no integral computed. That derivation is the test of the
anchor, not a restatement of it.

### 2. Filtration

**Needs** nothing.

"The information available at time $t$" is a slogan. What has to be true for it to be a
mathematical statement, and why is it a family of $\sigma$-algebras rather than a family of random
variables that does the work?

**Anchor.** Doob–Dynkin: $X$ is $\sigma(Y)$-measurable exactly when $X$ factors through $Y$. So
"measurable with respect to" is "factors through the map" — which is the tensor-notes intuition
about structure carried along a map, made exact. Check whether that reading survives a filtration
generated by a whole process rather than one variable.

### 3. Martingale

**Needs** 1, 2. **[live]** — already in [questions.md](questions.md#live).

What breaks if you keep only "conditional expectation of the future equals the present"? The
question as posed there: find the problem that forces the definition, before touching optional
stopping.

**Trap to watch.** The fair-game story makes the definition feel obvious and hides the filtration,
which is the half that is actually load-bearing — a process can be a martingale with respect to one
filtration and not another.

## Layer 2 — continuous-time Markov processes

Where the HMM anchor stops being enough. This layer is the real prerequisite for the chemical
master equation, and skipping it is why the CME usually arrives as a formula to accept.

### 4. Markov property in continuous time

**Needs** 1.

I hold the discrete-time, discrete-state case cold through HMMs. Continuous time breaks the
"one step" that the forward algorithm is built from. What replaces a transition matrix when there
is no next step, and what is left of Chapman–Kolmogorov?

**Anchor.** The HMM forward recursion *is* repeated multiplication by a transition matrix. That
is the object continuous time removes, so the node's real question is what a one-parameter family
of such matrices has to satisfy when there is no smallest step.

### 5. Jump chain and holding times

**Needs** 4.

Why must the holding time be exponential — which part of the Markov property forces memorylessness,
and is it forced or assumed? Then the question that matters downstream: what exactly is being given
up by a model that wants a non-exponential holding time, and how does the delay CME buy it back?

**Anchor.** Birth–death processes ([profile.md](profile.md#anchors-worth-building-on)): several
reactions compete, each with its own clock, and the next event is the minimum of them. Whether
that minimum-of-competing-clocks picture is the *content* of the construction or just a picture is
what this node decides.

**Conjecture to test.** If it is the content, node 10 should follow with nothing new added.
Check that there rather than assuming it here.

### 6. Generator and semigroup

**Needs** 1, 4, 5. **[live]** — already in [questions.md](questions.md#live).

The live question as posed: what do generators and semigroups buy over writing the master equation
out directly? Sharpened for this path — the generator is a derivative at $t=0$ and the semigroup is
its exponential, so this is the spectral story again on an infinite-dimensional space. Which parts
of that story survive, and which need boundedness I do not have?

**Anchor.** Matrix exponentials and spectral decomposition, held deeply. The gap is what fails when
the operator is unbounded and the state space is countably infinite.

### 7. Dynkin's formula and the martingale problem

**Needs** 3, 6.

Where the Layer 1 branch pays off, or fails to. A generator turns a process into a supply of
martingales, to the point that the process can be characterised by which functions it makes into
one. Is that a reformulation or a genuinely different handle — and does it give me anything for
jump processes on a countable state space specifically?

**Decision point.** If the honest answer is "a reformulation I do not need", Layer 1 was
a detour for this destination and the path should say so here.

## Layer 3 — the chemical master equation

Sources: van Kampen, [Stochastic Processes in Physics and
Chemistry](resources/stochastic-processes.md), and the primary papers in [CME and stochastic
transcription](resources/cme-transcription.md).

### 8. The CME as a Kolmogorov forward equation

**Needs** 6.

Given a mechanism — reaction channels with propensities — what determines the generator, and what
is the step where chemistry becomes mathematics? Specifically: propensity $\times\,dt$ as a
transition probability is an assumption about well-mixedness and timescale, not a definition.
What does it cost?

Second half of the node, and the thing Layer 4 expands around: in what sense, and under what
limit, do the mean dynamics reduce to the deterministic rate equations of mass action — and is
that limit a theorem or a closure assumption that happens to hold for first-order reactions?

**Conjecture to test.** If node 6 landed, the CME should turn out to be nothing new — just the
forward equation for a particular $Q$. Whether it is that cheap is the test of the live generators
question, so do not assume it going in.

### 9. Generating functions

**Needs** 8.

An infinite system of coupled ODEs becomes a single first-order PDE — in one variable per
species, so two for the splicing model at node 16. What property of the reaction network makes
that collapse work, and precisely where does it fail? The answer is the dividing line between the
models with closed-form solutions and the ones without, and it decides which models the field
uses.

**Anchor.** Transforms turning convolution into multiplication — the same move as Fourier, on a
different algebra.

**Source.** Jahnke & Huisinga 2007 and Singh & Bokes 2012, in
[resources/index.md](resources/cme-transcription.md).

### 10. Gillespie SSA and $\tau$-leaping

**Needs** 5, 8. **Source.** Gillespie 1976 and 2001, in
[resources/index.md](resources/cme-transcription.md).

The SSA is called exact. Exact in what sense — it is still a Monte Carlo estimator with a variance,
so what is exact and what is not? Then: $\tau$-leaping trades exactness for speed at a specific
place; find the place before reading where the paper says it is.

**Connects to.** The verification half of
[mathematical modelling](topics/mathematical-modelling.md#verification-vs-validation) — SSA is how
an analytic CME solution gets checked, and confusing that with validation is the error the topic
file warns about.

### 11. Finite state projection

**Needs** 8. **Source.** Munsky & Khammash 2006, in
[resources/index.md](resources/cme-transcription.md).

Truncating an infinite state space is an approximation of a kind I have no intuition for. What
makes the FSP error bound one-sided, and why is that the property that makes the method usable?

## Layer 4 — approximations, and what each one discards

Three routes lead from the CME to something continuous, and they are usually presented as
alternatives without saying whether they agree.

### 12. System-size expansion, LNA, chemical Langevin

**Needs** 8 — specifically its deterministic-limit half, which is what all three of these
expand around. Node 9 is a cross-check here, not a prerequisite.

Van Kampen's $\Omega$-expansion, Gillespie's two conditions for the CLE, and a truncated
Kramers–Moyal expansion all produce a diffusion. Do they produce the *same* diffusion, and if not,
what distinguishes them? What is the small parameter in each, and which one is actually small in a
cell with tens of mRNA molecules?

**Trap to watch.** "Fokker–Planck and Langevin are the same content in two languages" is a claim I
would repeat without being able to derive either direction. It goes on the path as something to
derive, not to accept.

**Sources.** Gillespie 2000, Vastola & Holmes 2020 and Thomas et al. 2014, in
[resources/index.md](resources/cme-transcription.md).

### 13. Poisson representation

**Needs** 8, 9.

Writing the solution as a *mixture* of Poissons — over a weight that is allowed to be negative or
complex, which is the part that makes it strange — looks like a trick. Why is it the natural
ansatz rather than a lucky one, and what does the weight being a quasi-probability actually cost?

**Anchor.** The negative binomial I fit daily is a gamma mixture of Poissons, i.e. this
representation with a weight that happens to be an honest density. So the node's real question is
what the mechanism has to look like for the weight to stay honest, and what it means when it does
not.

**Source.** Gardiner & Chaturvedi 1977, in
[resources/index.md](resources/cme-transcription.md).

### 14. Noise decomposition

**Needs** 1, 12. This is the node where node 1 is unambiguously load-bearing — the split is the
law of total variance, conditioning on the extrinsic variable — so if node 7 comes out a
reformulation, Layer 1 still earns its place here.

Intrinsic versus extrinsic noise is normally *defined* by a two-reporter experiment. What is the
model-level definition, and is it the same object? Then: a fluctuation–dissipation argument gives
second-order noise without solving the CME — what has to hold for that to be legitimate?

**Sources.** Paulsson 2004 and Gorin & Pachter 2020, in
[resources/index.md](resources/cme-transcription.md).

## Layer 5 — transcription models

### 15. Telegraph model and the bursty limit

**Needs** 9.

The bursty model is a limit of the telegraph model. Which limit, taken how, and why does it produce
a negative binomial — the distribution I fit routinely without being able to derive it? A model
whose stationary distribution I can derive from mechanism is a different object from one I fit.

**Sources.** Peccoud & Ycart 1995 and Shahrezaei & Swain 2008, in
[resources/index.md](resources/cme-transcription.md).

### 16. Unspliced and spliced as a two-species CME

**Needs** 9, 15.

Why does the joint $(U, S)$ distribution identify parameters that the $S$ marginal alone cannot?
This is the question underneath RNA velocity and underneath Monod, and it is an identifiability
question rather than a biological one.

**Source.** Gorin & Pachter 2022 on bursty transcription and splicing, in
[resources/index.md](resources/cme-transcription.md).

## Layer 6 — measurement, and the part that is mine

The layer where this path stops being reading and starts being my own work: the biophysical model
is upstream of a sequencing pipeline whose noise I understand better than its authors assume.

### 17. Technical noise as a thinning layer

**Needs** 15, 16.

Capture efficiency composes with the biophysical distribution. Under which compositions does the
family stay closed — and at what point does the technical layer become non-identifiable from the
biological one? Chemistry inference and the QC judgements made daily are exactly the prior that
this question needs.

**Sources.** Tang et al. 2020 and 2023, in
[resources/index.md](resources/cme-transcription.md).

### 18. Inference from a CME model

**Needs** 16, 17.

The likelihood needs the CME solution, so the set of inferable models is fixed by Layer 3 rather
than by biology. Does that constraint drive model choice in this field more than mechanism does —
and is that a compromise or the correct response to identifiability?

**Anchor.** Convex optimisation (EE364A) is held; the missing half is formulation, which is the
same gap named in [mathematical modelling](topics/mathematical-modelling.md#attached-to).

**Source.** Tang et al. 2023 and Gorin, Vastola, Fang & Pachter 2022 on tractability as a
modelling criterion, in
[resources/index.md](resources/cme-transcription.md).

## What this path deliberately leaves out

Named so that their absence is a decision rather than an oversight:

- **Measure theory for its own sake.** Only what nodes 1–3 need. If a construction demands more,
  that is a discovery to record, not a gap to pre-empt.
- **Brownian motion and stochastic integration.** The destination is jump processes on a countable
  state space. Itô calculus arrives only if node 12 turns out to need it.
- **Statistics proper** — DeGroot, Cox, and the inference-meaning direction in
  [profile.md](profile.md#current-directions). A real second direction, deliberately not
  interleaved here; node 18 is where the two would meet.
- **PDE technique.** Node 9's generating-function PDEs are solved by characteristics and little
  else. A PDE course is not on the way to anything here.
